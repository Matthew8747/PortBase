Ink lab — pick the density

"Flat" was the right word, and the cause was the medium, not the shape: a translucent shape on paper has nothing to be deep in. So the hero moved into the dark, and the two inks are now suspended in it — layered at different distances, blooming the way light does when it scatters, with grain in the medium itself. Same two colours, same rule. Three densities below; Bloom is mounted above.

MOUNTED ABOVE

Bloom

Five layers, broad and slow. The inks read as volume — masses of light with soft edges, crossing into a hot core where they meet.

Why this one: most depth for least noise. It fills the frame without competing with your name, which is still the loudest thing on the page.

ALTERNATE

Veil

Twenty-one thin sheets instead of five thick ones — closer to light through slats. More structure, more obviously engineered.

Trade-off: the closest of the three to the beams reference you sent, and for that reason the most likely to look like something you've seen before.

ALTERNATE

Filament

Four tight strands with more turns — nearer to the helix in the gutter, drawn at architectural scale.

Trade-off: the strongest link to the scroll motif, but sparse enough that the hero leans almost entirely on the typography.

On the two components you sent

Neither one, as written — and it's worth being precise about why, because the instinct behind sending them was right.

Aether Flow is a particle-network canvas with a purple glow, a pill badge and gradient-clipped text. That's the single most-generated hero on the internet right now, and connected-dot fields are on the banned list in DESIGN.md.

Ethereal Beams ships glassmorphic nav pills, a shimmer sweep on the buttons, gradient text, and invented statistics — "1M+ users", "Trusted by industry leaders". On your site those would be lies.

What's genuinely good in the second one is the technique: volumetric light with real falloff. That's what's above — rebuilt in your two inks, with no dependencies, no fake metrics, and your typography left in charge.

Restart Lab
  • Numbers gone. "01" implied a ranking that carried no information. The label now says what kind of work it is, which a reader actually needs.
  • Mono demoted to its real job. It appears only where the content is genuinely code or data — tech stacks, dates — never as a decorative label. Uppercase-mono-in-red was the tell you spotted.
  • The italic serif does the labelling instead, tying the small type back to the display face. One voice, two sizes, rather than three unrelated fonts on one row.
  • Real face named: Iowan Old Style / Palatino stack, not Georgia. Fraunces and Newsreader still get proper specimens in the type-system pass.

Also new in v4

  • Experience section — a real editorial timeline: Kontron, NY Racing, Secure Thingz, written as sentences rather than bullet fragments.
  • Footer designed — three columns, a closing statement, a colophon. Not a copyright line.
  • Header compresses on scroll — 78px to 60px, hairline and blur appear only once you've left the hero.
  • Radius cut to 2px across buttons and frames. Rounded corners were the softest, most default thing on the page.
  • Project frames carry a hatched ground so the placeholder reads as a plate awaiting artwork, not an empty div.

Deliberately not done

  • No testimonial slider, no logo wall, no metric counters. You have three real projects; the page's job is to make those unmissable.
  • No dark-mode toggle on this board — the site gets one, but the landing is composed for the warm ground first and inverted second, not designed twice at once.
  • Screenshots still placeholders. Real imagery is a build-phase task and will change the page's weight considerably.
